## Format to build toward

- Mobile-friendly motion comics using the game's ink and colored-pencil style: held panels, gentle camera movement, a few effects and short captions. Full animation or voice acting is not required for the first version.
- Opening target: 35–45 seconds. Levels 1–5 exits: 15–22 seconds each. Ending: 25–35 seconds. Optional final confrontation: 10–15 seconds. Rough target for the seven core scenes: about two-and-a-half to three minutes total, before player reading time.
- First thumbnail pass: six prologue panels, four panels for each of the first five exits, and six ending panels: **32 core panels**, plus three optional confrontation panels. These are planning counts, not a commitment to 32 separate image generations.
- One clear action or revelation per panel. Aim for no more than two short caption/dialogue lines at once; use names beside dialogue, readable type and strong contrast. Frame each important subject safely for both wide phones and tablets.
- Advance on Next/confirm; visible Skip, Back and destination controls are implemented for completed chapters. Replay through a future Story menu remains planned. Deaths do not repeat an already completed scene. Final pacing and persistent replay history remain later work.
- Sound accents: school ambience → impact/silence in the prologue; small fire crackles for mutation; distant bells in the Graveyard; machinery in the Furnace; a quiet pause for the reunion; engine noise into the flight transition. Keep captions sufficient to understand every scene with sound off.
- Companion pickups are optional. If a companion was not recruited, substitute a physical clue or recorded message for that companion's essential story action. Do not quietly grant a missing gameplay ability. Selected-hero runs also need a viewpoint variant rather than a second copy of the selected character in the same scene.

Continuity: the Dragon escapes this opening; its actual defeat belongs to the Level 1 fight. The worksheet goes back into X's pocket. Avoid narration that confirms his parents' deaths before that decision is made.

## C01 — After The Scratch: The Mark

Four panels; approximately 18 seconds. Source objects: the Flame Sword and a collection seal among the freight guarded by the Dragon. [Illustrated sequence](../../art/cutscenes/level1-v1/README.md).

1. **Aftermath:** the rooftop settles after the real boss defeat. X claims the established Flame Sword. “The Dragon was beaten. The Flame Sword was finally mine.” Use a clean composition that also works without optional helpers.
2. **Recognition:** close-up of the black-and-gold mark on a freight seal found among the crates the Dragon guarded; match it to the meteor rather than redesigning the actual sword. “That mark... I'd seen it before. It was carved into the meteor.”
3. **Reunion and a lead:** X meets his surviving schoolmate Barrett. Their familiar fist bump and relieved faces make their existing friendship clear. Barrett: “X! You made it too! I saw that mark on a Graveyard cart.” He shares something he observed after arriving, not lifelong native knowledge.
4. **Departure together:** the two friends look toward the moonlit Graveyard route. X: “Then the Graveyard is our next stop. We find answers. We find our friends.” No parents' rescue/death/location is implied.

New information: the meteor and the Dragon are connected. New objective: find the records. Do not reveal the launch authorization here; save that payoff for the Furnace.

## C02 — After The Graveyard: A World on a Page

Four panels; approximately 20 seconds. No post-defeat speech is required from Kilina's Daughter.

1. **The registry opens:** X and Barrett open a ledger of harvested worlds beside the Daughter's broken seal. Planet diagrams and inventory tally marks show resource extraction without individual casualties. “The Daughter's seal was broken. Inside: a ledger of harvested worlds.”
2. **Earthior:** X finds EARTHIOR beside a completed extraction mark and gold ingots. Other worlds fill the surrounding pages. “Earthior. Marked: extracted. Ours wasn't the only world they'd taken.” Authorization is not revealed in this copy.
3. **A familiar voice:** Barrett shares his radio; X recognizes surviving schoolmate Nico. “X? It's Nico! That tag's from the Furnace. Their launch records should still be there.” The physical shipment tag supplies the clue whether or not kind local helper Doghost was recruited. The clean illustration does not place a missing optional helper in the scene.
4. **A changed expression:** X folds the tag inside his worksheet as the Furnace glows on the horizon. “They didn't just take my world. The Furnace might tell us who did.”

New information: this was repeated, organized exploitation. New objective: reach the source records. The scene does not imply that every person whose world appears in the ledger died.

## C03 — After The Furnace: The Name

Four panels; approximately 20 seconds. Teleportation C is a schoolmate already encountered as an ally during this chapter; Kai and Ezra's appearance is another reunion with surviving Earthior pupils. No former-native or forced-service backstory is assumed.

1. **The furnace stops:** the Cinder Colossus's defeated shell dims. Ezra holds an open exit while X helps local creature workers and Kai recognizes their friend. Ezra: “X! Kai! You made it too! Help me get them clear of the machinery.”
2. **Recovered evidence:** Kai connects his cannon-arm interface to an undamaged logging terminal. The screen shows the marked meteor and launch path. “I can read the launch logs. This is the meteor that hit Earthior.”
3. **The answer:** the authorization resolves with the two prop headings **VOID REGENT / EARTHIOR** and an approval mark. X: “Void Regent. He signed the order. Now I have your name.” Faces/evidence carry the reveal instead of a screen of exposition.
4. **The next target:** C shares Kai's recovered chart, with a visible Witchwood target. “Witchwood is marked for the next strike. Kilina's charts may show his world.” C appears once; the chart supplies information without granting an uncollected companion/teleport ability.

New information: the Regent ordered it, and the machine is still operating. New objective: obtain the Dread Planet route at Kilina's sanctuary. X recognizes a repeated coordinate pattern; Kai does the technical decoding.

## C04 — After The Witchwood: Still Here

Four panels; approximately 22 seconds. The observatory records survive Kilina's actual defeat.

1. **The broken bargain:** X and C examine the contract and the same Witchwood target coordinates. X: “Kilina was promised protection. Her own home was marked for extraction.” No defeated-boss speech is required.
2. **The route:** C shares the recovered interplanetary chart. “The charts lead to Dread Planet. Nico has a ship ready for you.” The actual white/orange/lime Starbreak craft waits outside, providing physical travel rather than a required companion teleport.
3. **The familiar light:** a small stellar flare fades beside the sanctuary as Z-God arrives safely under her own power. Z: “X! I kept looking for you. I'm right here.” Her exact power origin is not established here.
4. **Reunion and boarding:** the siblings embrace beside the two-seat ship. X: “I was afraid I'd never see you again. Together. Whatever comes next.” Leave a quiet beat before engines start; previous schoolmate reunions remain meaningful. Actual flight boarding/launch follows Continue.

New information: his sister survived and has changed too. New objective: reach Dread Planet together. Her entrance is powerful and safe, never a scene of her needing rescue. Do not use this reunion to imply that the rest of Earthior has been restored.

## C05 — After Starbreak Run: The Next Impact

Four panels; approximately 16 seconds. Begin after the Pursuer's current wreck sequence, then use the established landing/disembark transition.

1. **The corridor clears:** the Pursuer's wreck is inert behind the Starbreak craft; Dread Planet fills the view. X: “The Pursuer fell behind us. Dread Planet filled the sky.” The actual wreck/landing/disembark have completed before the story opens.
2. **What paid for it:** X and Z compare an EARTHIOR cargo slip with the gold city. Kai over radio: “Earthior's gold. Their towers. They built this place from stolen worlds.” Other classmates help remotely rather than being crammed into the two-seat cockpit.
3. **Urgency:** the warning shows a meteor arming for the SAME Witchwood coordinates. Nico over radio: “He's arming another meteor. The target is still Witchwood.” No parents' location or unrelated third meteor plot is added.
4. **Touchdown:** X and Z face the distant launch structure from the landing path. X: “Then we stop it at the source. No more worlds. No more meteors.” Continue hands control back at the normal Level 6 start with the saved run state.

New information: the stolen resources built the Regent's power; the strike that threatens the refuge world is now imminent. New objective: reach the controls and the Regent. Witchwood is the breach site on the first four chapters' world, not a new planet or a separate third meteor subplot.

## C06A — Optional before the final fight: The Price

Three panels; approximately 12 seconds. Recommended if the future cutscene system supports a one-time arena trigger.

1. The Regent stands among vaulted resource columns and diagrams of world routes. “Do you know how many worlds pass through here?”
2. X produces the Earthior record. Regent: “I don't remember your world.”
3. X draws the Flame Sword. “Then I'm going to make sure you remember its name.” Match the last composition to the real arena's opening stance.

Purpose: let the antagonist reveal his indifference in his own voice. Keep this before combat so the ending does not require the dead/defeated Regent to deliver a monologue. In alternate-hero runs, the confrontation needs an ensemble or viewpoint variant rather than pretending that hero is Earthior's X.

Six panels, now illustrated and integrated; approximately 30 seconds. Start after the real final-boss defeat and exit. No resurrection or secret superior villain.

Resolution: the culprit is defeated, the next launch is prevented, and X chooses a purpose beyond the final blow. Earthior's loss remains meaningful. His playable schoolmates are confirmed survivors. His parents' fate stays unknown through this ending and may remain unresolved afterward; a future dark-planet rescue is a possibility, not a promised scene or a reveal placed here.
