# The Unknown Universe — story bible, draft 1

Status: proposed story development for review. The origin supplied by the creator is the foundation; new faction names, histories, connections and dialogue below are proposals. This document does not add cutscenes or alter gameplay.

Companion document: [cutscene beats and production plan](cutscene-beats-v1.md).

Creator clarifications: [authoritative story facts](creator-canon.md). All playable heroes are surviving Earthior schoolmates; the other helpers are kind creatures from the Unknown Universe. These facts supersede the earlier proposed native-character histories.

## The foundation we preserve

- X God was an ordinary child on **Earthior**. He liked school, his friends, math homework and being a good son to his parents.
- A huge meteor struck Earthior, destroyed his home planet and tore dimensional time. The rupture sent him into the Unknown Universe.
- He landed in a strange alley. A monster set him on fire. This unusual fire mutated him into one of the strongest beings in that universe and gave him access to fire, ice and other elemental powers.
- Evil beings deliberately sent the meteor to steal Earthior's gold and resources for their own planet.
- X God sets out to find the person responsible and get revenge.
- Creator decision: **his parents' individual fates remain unknown during the journey; an ending or later reveal is undecided and may never happen**. A future dark-planet rescue is only a possibility. Images and dialogue must not confirm deaths, captivity, a location or a reunion.
- All playable heroes are his Earthior schoolmates who were transported and survived. Teleportation C is also an ally; local creature helpers choose to help because of their shared kindness.

Existing game facts to respect: Z-God is his little sister and is the strongest, invincible character; seven heroes are selectable; the current campaign has six levels, ending with Void Regent on the Dread Planet. Dinio appears during Level 1, Doghost during Level 2, and Teleportation C during Level 3.

## The central story

An ordinary boy survives the destruction of his planet and becomes dangerously powerful in an unfamiliar universe. Following the people who profited from his loss, he discovers that Earthior was one entry in an ongoing planetary harvesting operation. His search for revenge becomes a fight to stop the next impact.

His emotional journey is **survive → find someone to blame → uncover the operation → reconnect → choose what his strength is for**. Defeating the culprit remains part of the resolution. Protecting others does not require forgiving him.

## Expanded origin

Before anyone called him X God, his biggest problems could fit on a sheet of homework. There were games to play after school, friends to meet the next morning and a family expecting him home. Those details matter: the planet he loses must feel like somewhere he belonged, rather than a disposable opening explosion.

The meteor arrives with something unnatural embedded in its surface: a black-and-gold mark. When it strikes, the horizon folds. Streets, sky and fragments of Earthior are pulled toward the same wound in space. X falls through with a folded math worksheet still in his pocket.

He wakes in an alley beneath the rooftops of **The Scratch**. The buildings look almost familiar, but the shadows and creatures do not. He calls for his family. Something answers from above.

**Proposed connection: that creature is Dark Dragon**, the existing first boss. A Court enforcer, it burns intruders and anything left outside the collection routes. Its attack is intended to kill X. It is not a secret lesson or a planned gift.

But the dimensional rupture has already changed the boy. The Dragon's stolen, primordial flame catches inside that change. His hair turns lime green; the crossed marks of his new eyes and his impossible strength appear. He survives the fire. At first, survival frightens him as much as the monster does.

His first important choice is small. He can chase the Dragon immediately, or stop to help Dinio, another creature caught in the chaos. He helps. The kid who tried to be a good son is still there beneath the mutation and anger.

The campaign begins with X moving through the school district's alleys and rooftops, learning what his body can do and finding the Dragon again. Defeating it gives him the Flame Sword and the first physical clue: the same black-and-gold mark he saw on the meteor.

## The enemy: the Gilded Court

**Proposed faction name: the Gilded Court.** It is a ruling circle that measures other worlds by what can be extracted from them. Void Regent heads the operation and personally authorized the strike on Earthior. He is the responsible final villain of this campaign, rather than a subordinate revealed to be serving a new villain at the ending.

Their meteor weapons do two jobs. The impact breaks a planet apart; the resulting dimensional wound becomes an extraction route. Gold, metals, elemental deposits and useful fragments of land are pulled toward their collection network and ultimately the Dread Planet. Survivors are an inconvenience to be contained, exploited or erased from the records.

Gold is both wealth and a conductor for their machinery. This makes their greed visible: the final world's gilded structures contain material stolen from places like Earthior. The Court could use its technology to trade or transport resources. Destruction is a profitable choice, not a tragic necessity.

The network has distinct functions: the Dragon patrols collection territory; the Graveyard inventories what was lost; the Furnace refines the stolen resources; Kilina supplies dimensional routes; the fleet protects delivery; the Dread Planet issues the next launch order. Each level exposes another part of the same crime.

Geography proposal: the first four chapters occupy regions of one collection/refuge world in the Unknown Universe; Starbreak carries the crew to the separate Dread Planet. Witchwood is the next meteor's intended breach site on that refuge world, not a new planet name. The Court plans to abandon its outpost and harvest what remains, betraying Kilina and threatening the people X has just met. The Furnace targeting record and the flight black box refer to this same planned strike.

Void Regent's defining cruelty is indifference. He did not need to hate Earthior to destroy it. He approved a number on a page.

Proposed confrontation:

> **Regent:** “I don't remember your world.”
>
> **X God:** “Then I'm going to make sure you remember its name.”

## Powers and limits

**Proposed name: the First Fire.** It is raw elemental energy, from before fire, cold and electrical force were separated into stable forms. The Court uses it industrially; Dark Dragon carries a weaponized form.

The fire gives X the potential to handle several elements. It does not give him instant mastery. The Flame Sword, Frost Gauntlet, Ember Boots and Storm Halo help him focus that potential into usable forms. This supports the current weapon progression without treating each pickup as an unrelated miracle. Damage can overload a focus or knock it out of use; it does not undo his mutation.

X survives because the rupture and flame interact in an unusual way. There is no prophecy that required his planet to die, and no secret plan to create him. The Court made a survivor it cannot control.

His strength does not supply the culprit's name, the right coordinates or the ability to protect everyone at once. His habits from Earthior still help him: he notices repeated numbers and symbols, keeps track of routes, and refuses to treat frightened strangers as disposable. Kai handles engineering; X does not suddenly become an expert scientist because he enjoyed math.

Z-God's strength must remain real. Do not give her a convenient weakness, drain her powers, imprison her behind arbitrary magic, or make X secretly stronger. Invincibility does not reveal hidden information or put her everywhere at once. While X follows records, she follows survivors through the rupture's aftermath. In her playable run, she can defeat bosses as easily as the game promises.

The first story pass follows X God's journey, with the other playable heroes as surviving school friends rather than unrelated recruits. Alternate-hero runs can follow a different member of that same survivor group; X's specific fire-mutation scene still belongs to X. Decide viewpoint variants before writing seven versions of every cutscene. Reunions can first appear in illustrations or transmissions; no new roaming NPC system is assumed.

Companion pickups are currently optional. Essential clues must still work if one was missed: X can find Doghost's shipment tag himself, and recorded gate instructions can supply C's route knowledge. A cutscene cameo must not silently award a companion or ability. The eventual scripts need small presence/viewpoint variants, including avoiding a duplicate Teleportation C when he is the selected hero.

## Ending proposal

Regent is defeated decisively. His final attack leaves the harvesting controls unstable and the next meteor still armed. X can keep striking an already beaten enemy, or turn toward the people and worlds still at risk. He turns. That choice does not undo the boss's defeat or let him escape justice.

Kai isolates the launch circuit, C opens a safe local evacuation path, and the others defend it. Z-God holds up a collapsing structure while people escape, showing overwhelming power used generously. X helps sever the weapon's elemental conduits and speaks to the Regent before the machine finally goes dark:

> “Earthior wasn't yours to spend.”

The meteor launch stops. The Court's weapon network is broken. Earthior remains destroyed; this ending does not magically reset the loss or reveal that none of it was real. Its stolen resources can help build homes and repair damaged places instead of another throne.

In a quiet final image, X unfolds the scorched worksheet from the opening. On its back he sketches the route to the next group of survivors. The boy who liked solving problems is still inside the hero.

Possible closing caption: **“They took his world. They don't get the next one.”**

Do not introduce a larger secret mastermind in the final seconds. A later game can explore the damaged universe without taking this victory away.

## Tone and visual continuity

- Warm, strange, hand-drawn adventure with serious loss and playful personalities. No graphic depiction of Earthior's casualties is needed.
- Treat Earthior, its people and X's grief sincerely. Put humor in personalities, weird creatures and improvised gear.
- Three recurring visual anchors: the folded worksheet; the meteor/Court's split-circle mark; the contrast between warm, remembered home and cold hoarded gold. The Court mark is a proposed new prop design.
- The pencil-and-ink look stays consistent with the existing character art. It does not imply that the world is imaginary or that X dreamed the destruction.
- The lore should explain the mission, not replace action with lectures. Each exit scene answers one question and opens one new objective.

## Decisions to settle before final art

1. Are **Gilded Court**, **First Fire** and **Harvest Engine** the final terms? The last is a working label for the machinery, not a second villain.
2. Is Dark Dragon definitely the alley attacker? This version recommends it because it gives the first fight a strong payoff.
3. What are X and Z's ages and original names, if they have names beyond their hero names? Leave these unspecified for now.
4. Parents' fates stay unknown during the journey. Whether the ending or a later story resolves them remains open; the current draft leaves them unresolved. A future dark-planet rescue is only a possibility. All playable schoolmates are confirmed survivors; other people's individual fates remain unspecified.
5. Does the proposed stellar-rupture origin fit Z-God? Her sibling relationship, invincibility and superior strength are already established.
6. Do ordinary boss defeat animations mean literal death in the story? Until decided, exit scenes recover records and objects rather than requiring defeated bosses to stand up and deliver dialogue.

Once those choices are settled, the next production step is thumbnail storyboards for the prologue and Level 1 exit, followed by the other five exits. Existing gameplay and the pending roster-code work remain unchanged by this writing pass.
